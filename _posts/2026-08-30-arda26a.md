abstract: " Understanding why Transformers perform so well remains challenging due
  to their non-convex optimization landscape. In this work, we analyze a shallow Transformer
  with $m$ independent heads trained by projected gradient descent in the kernel regime.
  Our analysis reveals two main findings: (i) the width required for nonasymptotic
  guarantees scales only logarithmically with the sample size $n$, and (ii) the optimization
  error is independent of the sequence length $T$. This contrasts sharply with recurrent
  architectures, where the optimization error can grow exponentially with $T$. The
  trade-off is memory: to keep the full context, the Transformer’s memory requirement
  grows with the sequence length. We validate our theoretical results numerically
  in a teacher–student setting and compare Transformers with recurrent architectures
  on an autoregressive task. "

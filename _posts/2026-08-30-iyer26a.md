abstract: " Generating samples from exponentially tilting a given distribution of
  random vectors when samples from the given distribution are available finds applications
  in fields such as finance and climate science and in the broad area of rare event
  simulation. In this article, we discuss the asymptotic efficiency of an estimator
  obtained by exponentially tilting the empirical distribution. We provide a sharp
  characterization of how much one can accurately tilt distributions given a certain
  number of samples. Our findings reveal a surprising dichotomy: While twisting unbounded
  distributions is a fundamentally hard task, for bounded distributions, one can accurately
  tilt by a large amount using much fewer samples. "

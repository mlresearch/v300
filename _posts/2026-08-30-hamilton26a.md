abstract: " Two-point correlation functions (2PCF) are widely used to characterize
  how points cluster in space. In this work, we study the problem of measuring the
  2PCF over a large set of points, restricted to a subset satisfying a property of
  interest. An example comes from astronomy, where scientists measure the 2PCF of
  star clusters, which make up only a tiny subset of possible sources within a galaxy.
  This task typically requires careful labeling of sources to construct catalogs,
  which is time-consuming. We present a human-in-the-loop framework for efficient
  estimation of 2PCF of target sources. By leveraging a pre-trained classifier to
  guide sampling, our approach adaptively selects the most informative points for
  human annotation. After each annotation, it produces unbiased estimates of pair
  counts across multiple distance bins simultaneously. Compared to simple Monte Carlo
  approaches, our method achieves substantially lower variance while significantly
  reducing annotation effort. We introduce a novel unbiased estimator, sampling strategy,
  and confidence interval construction that together enable scalable and statistically
  grounded measurement of two-point correlations in  astronomy datasets. "

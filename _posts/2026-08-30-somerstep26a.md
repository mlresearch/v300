abstract: " Using the bit string generation problem as a case study, we theoretically
  compare two standard methods for adapting large language models to new tasks. The
  first, referred to as \\emph{supervised fine-tuning}, involves training a new next
  token predictor on good generations. The second method, \\emph{Best-of-N}, trains
  a reward model to select good responses from a collection generated by an unaltered
  base model. If the learning setting is realizable, we find that supervised fine-tuning
  outperforms BoN through a better dependence on the response length in its rate of
  convergence. If realizability fails, then depending on the failure mode, BoN can
  enjoy a better rate of convergence in either $n$ or a rate of convergence with better
  dependence on the response length. "

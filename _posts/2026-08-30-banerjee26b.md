abstract: " Function approximation with parametric, feature-based reward models is
  widely used to enable decision-making in bandits with large action spaces. While
  bandit learning is well understood in the case of little or no misspecification
  in the reward approximation, real-world applications can often involve significantly
  high model misspecification. We study whether optimal learning is still possible
  under arbitrary misspecification. We identify structural, instance-dependent conditions,
  determined jointly by the problem instance and model class, under which standard
  algorithms like $\\epsilon$-greedy and LinUCB achieve sublinear regret, despite
  an arbitrarily large misspecification error in the traditional sense. These results
  contrast sharply with worst-case analyses that predict linear regret, and show that
  a broad class of instances remains robust to model error. Our findings offer a theoretical
  explanation for the empirical success of approximate value-based methods in complex
  environments. "
